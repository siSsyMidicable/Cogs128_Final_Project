## Participant 1 — Alex (21, College Student)

**Me:** "Okay so first question have you ever traded skills informally before? Like not using money, just helping each other out?"

**Alex:** "Yeah, honestly I didn't even think of it as like 'trading skills' at the time, but yeah. Like last semester I helped my friend fix his resume because I'm kinda good with formatting and wording, and then he helped me study for calc. I was struggling a lot and he's way better at math than me."

**Me:** "So it was kind of like an unspoken exchange?"

**Alex:** "Yeah exactly, like we didn't sit down and say 'this for that,' it just kinda happened naturally. But looking back, it was definitely a trade."

**Me:** "What would make you trust someone enough to do that with like… a random person instead of a friend?"

**Alex:** "I think that's the hard part. With friends it's easy because you already know them. With a random person, I'd want to see like… proof they actually know what they're doing. Like past work, maybe reviews, something like that."

**Me:** "Yeah that makes sense. And how would you decide if something is fair?"

**Alex:** "I don't think it has to be like perfectly equal, but I'd want to feel like I didn't get the worse end of it. Like if I spend a lot of time helping someone, I'd expect them to put in effort too. Effort matters more than exact value to me."

**Me:** "Do you think you'd use an app for that kind of thing?"

**Maria:** "I would, but only if I felt like the people on it were legit."

**Me:** "What makes someone feel legit to you?"

**Me:** "Yeah that's fair. And how do you personally decide if a trade is fair?"
